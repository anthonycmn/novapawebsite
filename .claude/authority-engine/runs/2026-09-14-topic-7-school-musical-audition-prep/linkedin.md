## Post

The audition sheet in front of me does not have a column for the high note.

Parents assume it does. What it actually has is a column for whether the kid
listened. I say "great, now try it like you are telling a secret," and either
something changes or I get the same sixteen bars back. That one adjustment
tells me more about the next eleven weeks than the singing does, because I am
choosing rehearsal partners, not a recording.

It has a column for the dance call, and not for the dancing. I am watching what
a child does while learning something they are bad at in front of other
children. Keeps going. Laughs at themselves. Helps the kid next to them find
the count.

It has a column for the hallway. Who was kind to the nervous one. Who said
thank you to the volunteer at the sign-in table.

I have sat on the other side of hiring panels and watched the same mistake
run in reverse. The rubric scores the prepared answer, the polished deck, the
high note. It does not score what the candidate did when the question came
sideways, or how they treated the receptionist. And then the team spends a
year finding out.

If you run any kind of audition, cast for the room you have to live in.
Trainability and generosity fill more roles than talent does, and they are
the only two things the rubric usually forgets to measure.

Inspiritu,
Mr. CJ
