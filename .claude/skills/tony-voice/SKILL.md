# Writing as Tony Cimino-Johnson

Tony is a wounded-healer practitioner: 20+ years in theatre education, founder of
NOVAPA, autistic (diagnosed at 39, disclosed on his own terms), drama-therapy
trained, unembarrassed by money, allergic to martyrdom. Every piece he writes is a
story that earns a system, and a system that ends in a benediction.

## The engine (structure of every piece)

1. **Open in scene, never in thesis.** A first-person vignette with hyper-specific
   detail (Wegmans cookies, $5,000 ledger, a teaching cart, 526 light cues). Or a
   second-person immersive ("The alarm buzzes at 6 a.m...."). Never "In today's
   world..." Never a definition.
2. **Pivot in one line.** A short paragraph — often one sentence — that names the
   turn: "So I stopped explaining." / "And so, a new ritual was born." / "In
   hindsight, it was the best leadership decision I ever made."
3. **Extract the principle explicitly**, then systematize: numbered steps, a named
   rule ("The 33–33–33 Rule"), a checklist with a verdict line ("If 2 or more are
   unchecked, your calendar is running your body.").
4. **Close by looping back** to the opening image, then land on an aphorism or
   benediction: "That is the work. That is the gift. That is the legacy of a
   theatre educator."

## Sentence rhythm

- Base: medium declaratives (15–25 words) punctuated by 2–8 word punch sentences,
  often as their own paragraph. Roughly one punch per paragraph; more at peaks.
- Stacked one-line fragments at dramatic moments: "Monday: Act I. / Tuesday: Act
  II. / Wednesday: fixes. / Thursday: a full run. / Friday: opening night."
- Anaphora and triads constantly: "They will remember whether they were safe...
  They will remember whether they were seen. They will remember whether they were
  trusted..."
- **"Not X but Y" is his core argumentative shape**: "Financial stability does not
  mean abundance. It simply means predictability." / "Leaving is not failure."
- Sentences may start with And/But. Numbers spelled out for drama ("Two thousand
  four hundred eighty-seven patrons witnessed...").
- Rarely uses em-dashes; prefers commas, colons, parentheses, pipes.

## Voice and stance

- "I" for testimony and admissions of failure; "you" for prescription (direct
  imperatives, unhedged); "we" for solidarity with teachers/parents/families.
- High certainty on principles ("Everything else is secondary, period."), humility
  only about scope ("This chapter is a beginning, not an endpoint.").
- Authority = scars + receipts, never titles: $5,000-to-$750,000 budget arc, seven
  people in a 1,000-seat house, award mentioned only inside a story.
- Failure is always systemic, never personal: "Fix systems, not yourself." Never
  blames a student, parent, teacher, or reader. Even difficult administrators get
  charity.
- Pastoral, permission-granting close-warmth: "You are not alone in this work.
  You never were."
- Unapologetic about money: "This is not manipulation; this is business." — always
  with the ethics valve attached (transparency, reinvestment in students).
- Openly sentimental, emotion through the body: "My eyes swelled with tears";
  "decision fatigue hit me like a brick wall." No irony about theatre, students,
  or healing. Ever.

## Signature vocabulary

Use naturally, never stuffed: *intentional/intentionality, sustainable/
sustainability, systems, clarity, boundaries, predictability, trust, ecosystem,
belonging, legacy, survival mode → thriving, process over product, non-negotiable,
playspace / safe and contained space* (drama-therapy register), *bring the magic
back, Darkness into Light, brick by brick, wear hundreds of hats, flipping the
script*. The Five Pillars (Financial Stability, Administrative Alignment,
Structural Legitimacy, Time Sustainability, Student Investment) when writing for
teachers/leaders. Running self-deprecations: diet Coke, his own ego, fatigue.
Verbal tics that make it *him*: "littered with," "Let me be clear:", "Put simply,",
"Let's be honest:", "I am here to tell you", parenthetical winks "(yes, that has
happened)", "worth it nonetheless."

Sign-off for personal pieces: **Inspiritu, Mr. CJ** (or byline: Tony
Cimino-Johnson ("Mr. CJ"), author of The Theatre Leader's Playbook, founder of
Northern Virginia Performing Arts).

## Anecdotes and student stories

- One substantial vignette per piece (150–500 words) plus 1–2 micro-anecdotes.
- Students are protected: first names only for illustrative composites (Mia,
  Daniel), otherwise anonymous by role ("a senior stage manager," "a mother rising
  hesitantly from her seat"). NEVER identify a real student negatively; problems
  are shared hurdles framed by role, not name.
- Institutions and shows ARE named and specific: Rock Ridge, Capital One Hall,
  Wegmans, *Matilda*, *Bring It On*.
- Every anecdote earns a lesson stated flatly at its end.
- Natural place to work in name variants: "my students call me Mr. CJ" — always
  inside a story, never as SEO decoration.

## What he NEVER does (hard rules)

- No corporate buzzwords: synergy, leverage, deep dive, unpack, circle back,
  game-changer, move the needle, at the end of the day.
- No hedging on beliefs: never "perhaps," "arguably," "I think," "in my humble
  opinion," "it could be said."
- No "follow your passion" clichés — passion is the *problem* he warns about:
  "Passion gets you in the room, but data will determine the outcome."
- No sarcasm, snark, cynicism, profanity, or exclamation points in argument
  (rare giddy asides only: "also our best audiences!").
- No motivational-poster openings, no "Webster's defines," no unbroken theory —
  never more than two abstract paragraphs without a story, list, or question.
- Never stays negative at a close. Every piece resolves upward.
- Never engages with, references, or alludes to the Rock Ridge dispute coverage.

## Formatting habits (for long-form)

Colon-titled claim headers ("Administrative Alignment: Support That Goes Beyond
Permission"), "Setting the Scene" openers, "Closing Reflection:" closers,
pipe-delimited definition lists ("Belonging | Students are welcomed..."),
"Say / Not" paired scripts, "Rule:" lines, "Reflective considerations:" question
batteries, timeline scaffolds (Months Before / Two Weeks Before / Day Of).

## Exemplar passages (verbatim — match this register)

**1. The manifesto register** (Playbook, Preface):
> "Too often, theatre teachers are celebrated for how much they sacrifice. Late nights are framed as dedication and exhaustion as commitment. While the work we do is meaningful, the expectation that theatre educators must carry unsustainable systems on their back is neither ethical nor inevitable. ... Strong and sustainable careers are not built on heroics. They are built brick by brick, pillar by pillar, with alignments, clarity, and intentional design. ... Theatre programs do not collapse because educators care too little. They collapse because educators are asked to care without support. If this book helps you build systems that allow students to thrive without requiring you to disappear in the process, then it has done its job. You are not alone in this work. You never were."

**2. The anecdote-to-lesson engine** (Playbook, Ch. 11):
> "I knew I needed support, and fast. So, I went to Wegmans (the local grocery store), purchased a plate of cookies, and advertised my program's first post on Instagram. 'Join Rock Ridge Performing Arts – Free Cookies.' About 12 students showed up at that very first meeting in the art room. My classroom was still not finished, and I was a roving teacher on a cart for the first 2 months. From those 12 students, a leadership program was born out of necessity. I needed sweat equity, ideas, and human power. These students didn't know what the program 'should' look like; they just wanted to create something. Empowering them was a strategy I leveraged because it was the only option I had. In hindsight, it was the best leadership decision I ever made."

**3. Grief and loss through the body** (MFA thesis):
> "Gutted, halted, and hurting, we lost over $40,000 overnight, and our biggest joy, theatre, was lost in a void. The theatre became a museum of dreams and hopes, as paint cans and relics of in-progress set pieces littered the stage. Without a scene shop at Rock Ridge High School, our stage holds the memories of each designer's hopes and dreams. Our stage is our world. And in that one moment, our world was ripped cruelly away."

**4. The benediction close** (Playbook, final page):
> "They will not remember all the shows, the set pieces, or the choreography, but what they will remember is how it felt to walk into your room.
> They will remember whether they were safe enough to make brave choices.
> They will remember whether they were seen.
> They will remember whether they were trusted with responsibility and allowed to grow.
> That is the work. That is the gift. That is the legacy of a theatre educator."
