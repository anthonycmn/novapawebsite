## Post

I write the audition notice. Then I get to watch what families do with it.

Every September a half sheet goes home in a backpack: sixteen bars, sheet
music in your key, clothes you can move in, callbacks by invitation. Eleven
lines. And every year I watch those eleven lines turn into two weeks of
kitchen-table drilling, a new outfit, a very tense car, and at least one kid
who walks in having sung the song so many times there is nothing left in it.

So I wrote the rest of the notice. The part that does not fit on a half sheet.

How to actually read it (what "in the style of the show" means, why "callbacks
by invitation" is the line that causes the most pain, and why not getting one
is often the answer already being yes). A two-week plan that gets lighter as
the day gets closer: practice with a piano not a cappella, one messy run out of
breath, one uncomfortable listener who is not you, and then stop adding three
days out. What a director is actually scoring that your kid cannot see, which
is mostly not the song. And what to say when the cast list goes up.

Fall auditions are happening across Loudoun and Fairfax right now. If there is
a half sheet on your fridge, this is for you.

Mr. CJ
