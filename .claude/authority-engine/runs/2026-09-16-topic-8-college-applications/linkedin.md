## Post

Every spring a junior sits across from me with a schedule request form and says: "I love theatre, but I think I should take another AP instead. It will look better."

For years my answer was an argument. Then I stopped arguing and built a system instead.

At Rock Ridge High School I developed Virginia's first Dual Enrollment Theatre Program, in partnership with Richard Bland College of William & Mary, so that a student in our theatre classes was earning college credit on a transcript while still in high school. A $32,000 grant from the Jack Kent Cooke Foundation later carried it into stagecraft. Some years, fifteen to twenty percent of the school was enrolled in a theatre course.

The theatre kids loved it. But the program was built for the other kids: the future nurse, the future engineer, the future lawyer who had been told, gently, that the thing they loved did not count. Now it counted on paper, in a room where they were already happy. Nobody had to choose.

That is the leadership lesson, and it is not about theatre. When the people you serve keep being forced to choose between what they love and what is rewarded, the answer is rarely a better speech. It is a structure that removes the choice.

Passion gets students in the room. Systems keep them there.

Inspiritu,
Mr. CJ
