## The Room Comes Down Before the Armor Does

A federal contractor joined our adult acting class last fall. He presents to
two hundred people for a living. On night one he told me he had never once felt
present while doing it. Eight weeks later he said the sentence I have been
carrying around since: "I stopped bracing."

I want to talk about that sentence, because it is the most useful thing I know
about teaching adults, and it applies to every room a leader ever walks into.

## The armor is the problem, not the person

Every adult beginner arrives with a highly developed system for controlling how
they are perceived. It took decades to build. It is invisible to the person
wearing it. And it is expensive: every unit of attention spent monitoring
yourself is a unit you cannot spend on the human in front of you.

That is why the presenter cannot feel present. He is not under-prepared. He is
over-defended. Preparation was never the missing piece.

## You cannot remove the armor by asking

I tried, for years. "Relax." "Just be yourself." "Nobody is judging you." Every
one of those sentences makes the armor thicker, because each one is a reminder
that someone is watching.

What actually works is a room. Specifically: a room where managing your image
is impossible, and where nothing bad happens when you fail to manage it. The
circle warm-up where everyone is doing the same ridiculous thing at once. The
counting game that occupies the whole brain. The two-line scene where the
words are so small there is nothing to get right. The armor comes down not
because I asked, but because the room stopped requiring it.

## The leadership translation

I run programs for five-year-olds through adults, and the adults need the room
most. Children have not built the armor yet. Adults carry theirs into every
meeting.

So the question for anyone who leads people is not "why is my team not
bringing their full attention." It is: what are they bracing against when I
walk in? Whose image are they managing? What would it take for that management
to become unnecessary?

You cannot tell people to stop bracing. You can only build a room where
bracing is pointless. That is design work. It is done brick by brick, cue by
cue, and it is the most important thing a leader does before the first word of
the agenda.

The parent-facing version of this, who actually takes adult acting classes,
whether you are too old, and what happens minute by minute on the first night, is
on the NOVAPA blog (link above). This is the part I wanted to say to the people
who run rooms for a living.

Fix the room, not the people.
