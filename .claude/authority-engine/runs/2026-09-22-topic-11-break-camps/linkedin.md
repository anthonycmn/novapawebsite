# LinkedIn — topic 11, break camps

The first email always arrives in October, and it is never about theatre.

"Hi — are you open on November 3?"

That's the whole message. Nothing about the curriculum, nothing about who's teaching. For a while that stung a little. I wanted the question to be about the work.

Then I counted our own calendar. Between the first teacher workday in October and the last day of spring break in March, the school divisions around us close for roughly eighteen weekdays. Nearly four working weeks in which school is out and work is not.

So I stopped treating the childcare question as the lesser question.

Here is what I think we get wrong in arts education when we build these days: we design the camp for the child and price it for the parent, and then feel vaguely disappointed when families book it for the date on the calendar rather than the art on the page. That disappointment is a failure of design, not of the family.

A break camp has to answer two questions at once. Does this solve my Tuesday, and is my kid going to have a real day? A program that answers only the first is childcare with a piano in the room. A program that answers only the second — ending at noon, with aftercare sold separately — has not actually solved anything.

Both, or neither. That is the whole standard.

The practical version, for anyone building these: one day is one skill. Five consecutive days is one show. Advertise outside that ratio and families feel the gap before they can name it, which is how a program quietly loses a second booking it never knew it lost.
