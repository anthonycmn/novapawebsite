Every July I stand in an empty rehearsal room on a Monday morning knowing that in fourteen days, a company of five-to-fifteen-year-olds will open a complete licensed musical for a paying public.

The first time I built that schedule, I assumed the constraint was the enemy. I was wrong. The constraint is the curriculum.

A two-week production camp is theatre education with the excuses removed. Belonging work first, casting inside forty-eight hours, music before staging, and a company that goes home rested on the eve of opening. The show opens either way. The question is whether the children were built up or used up on the way there.

This week I published an honest guide for parents comparing summer theatre camps in Northern Virginia — including the money questions our industry loves to hide behind "inquire for pricing." I publish prices. I wish more of us did. Transparent pricing is not a marketing choice. It is a respect choice.

Guide is in the comments. Fellow theatre educators: the dress-week system behind it is Chapter 14 of The Theatre Leader's Playbook.
