Late September, and the email always has the same shape.

"She decided in August that this is what she wants to do. Are we already too late?"

Every college audition timeline published online is written for a family who started in junior year, worked through the summer, and arrived in September with a book, a reel, and a list. That family exists. I have taught that family.

They are not the family typing that question into a search bar at eleven at night, and I think our field has quietly decided not to write for the second family at all.

Which is strange, because they are a large share of who actually shows up.

Two things I'd offer anyone advising students through this.

First, the deadline that shapes the year is the prescreen, not the audition. Most programs set prescreen dates between November 1 and December 15, and a live slot usually depends on that recording. A student planning backward from January is planning from the wrong month by roughly a semester. We know this. We do not say it early enough or loudly enough.

Second, when a student is behind, the instinct is to compress everything equally. That is the wrong move. Reduce the number of schools before you reduce the quality of anything — six done properly beats fifteen done in a hurry, and the one thing never to economise on is recording quality. Faculty forgive a modest voice. They do not forgive not being able to hear it.

The piece is in the comments. It runs two tracks, the student's and the parent's, because the parent timeline is real and almost nobody writes it down.
