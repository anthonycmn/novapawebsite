## Post

A mom stood in our lobby last year and asked me whether her son was too young to
start. He was eight. I get that question more than any other, and I have never
liked the way the internet answers it.

Eight months later he was standing in front of the Duloc doors in a red felt
crown and yellow tights, walking the whole show on his knees as Lord Farquaad,
and two hundred people laughed before he said a single word.

So here is the honest answer, from someone who has been building these rooms for
twenty years. Most kids are ready for a first theatre class somewhere between
five and seven — as long as that class is built on play rather than technique.
Real structured training tends to land between eight and ten, when they read
fluently, remember blocking week to week, and can take a note without hearing it
as a verdict on who they are. And if your child is twelve, or fifteen, and has
never set foot onstage? They are not late. I have watched students who started
at fifteen out-work students who started at five, every single season, because
they chose it themselves.

That "ages 8 to 11" number you keep running into online is a casting statistic.
It's about when a child can book professional work. It was never about when a
child should learn.

I wrote the whole thing up — what a five-year-old can actually do in a class,
what changes at nine and again at thirteen, five signs your child is ready, the
five questions to ask any program before you enroll, and what our classes
actually cost, because almost nobody around here publishes prices and I think
that's indefensible.

Forget the birthday. The right age is the age the room was built for.

— Mr. CJ
