## Caption

The first budget I was handed for a theatre program was $5,000. For the year.

We did what everyone does. Catalogs. A car wash. The discount card nobody wanted twice. I spent my Saturdays on it, and the next September the budget was still $5,000.

What eventually changed the number wasn't a better fundraiser. It was giving up on fundraising.

Fundraising is asking people for money and handing them something incidental. It comes once, late, with a vendor's cut taken out, and it builds nothing.

Revenue is what the program earns doing what it already does. Tickets priced like you believe in the work. Sponsorship. The building used in the summer instead of sitting dark. Same activity, twice.

And the test I'd give any teacher reading this: take the profit, count every hour it cost YOU, and divide. If that number is worse than the work it displaced, it isn't a fundraiser. It's a transfer of your life at a bad exchange rate.

Programs don't fail because they run out of money. They fail because you do.
