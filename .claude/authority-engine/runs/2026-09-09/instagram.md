She got eleven bars in, stopped, and asked: "Can I just sing the one I sing in the car?"

Nine years old. She'd walked in with a phone playing a lyric video, in a key written for a grown woman, because someone told her it would be impressive.

Then she sang the car song. Ordinary. In her range. She meant every word of it.

She was cast that night.

Twenty years behind the audition table and I can tell you what almost every "best audition songs for kids" list gets wrong: it ranks songs by how impressive they sound. We don't. We're listening for where the voice lives, whether a kid can hold a thought while a piano happens around them, and what they do in the half second after something goes wrong.

A hard song at 70% reads as a warning. A simple song at 100% reads as a professional.

New guide is up: what to sing at ages 6 to 13, the overdone list with honest context (spoiler: overdone isn't a disqualifier), and how to cut sixteen bars without wrecking the song.
