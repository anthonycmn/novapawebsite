Broadway Bound Teen • *Dear Evan Hansen* • Day 1
Monday, August 3

## Welcome to the company

Dear Broadway Bound Teens,

Tomorrow morning twenty-four of you walk into a room and become a company. By Friday the fourteenth you will have built, rehearsed, and performed one of the most demanding musicals written this century. Two weeks. That is the whole runway.

I want to be straight with you about what this one asks, because *Dear Evan Hansen* is not like the shows most of you have done with us. It is about a boy who dies by suicide and a boy who lies about having known him. It is about loneliness, mental illness, grief, and what people do when they are desperate to be seen. It is funny and it is devastating, sometimes in the same breath.

We chose it for you because you are ready for it, and because young people deserve serious material handled seriously. We will look after each other while we work on it. More on that below, and it is the part I most want you to read.

## Auditions are Monday

**If you did not audition in June, you audition on Day 1.** This is for placement only. Everyone who registered is in the company and everyone is cast. What Monday decides is where you fit best.

Bring:

- **A song you are confident singing.** Sixteen to thirty-two bars is plenty. It does not need to be from *Dear Evan Hansen*, and it does not need to be new. Bring sheet music in a binder, or a backing track on your phone with a way to play it.
- **A short monologue** if you have one you like. Optional, but it helps us.
- Yourself, warmed up, having slept.

If you auditioned in June, you do not need to prepare anything. You will be working while placements happen.

A word on this: an audition here is not a test you can fail. We already want you. We are listening for where your voice sits, what you are drawn to, and which room will stretch you the most.

## How Monday actually runs

- **9:00** Company call. Introductions, how we work, and the conversation about the material.
- **9:30 to 11:30** We read the entire show, out loud, all of us, before anyone stages anything. You will have the script in your hands and you will hear the whole story in one sitting. This is my favourite morning of any process.
- **11:30 to 12:00** We listen to the score and talk about the show together. Bring your questions and your arguments.
- **12:00 to 12:45** Lunch.
- **Afternoon** Scene work begins. Placements and auditions happen alongside it.
- **4:00** Pick-up.

## Everyone works, all day, every day

This is the part that surprises people new to Broadway Bound Teen. Nobody sits in a hallway on their phone waiting to be needed. Every one of you is in a named track for every block of every day, and the tracks rotate so you get all of it:

- **Stage** — blocking and scene work with Danielle
- **Music** — vocals, harmony, sectionals with Shelby
- **Costume, hair and makeup** — fittings, builds, makeup labs with Ryanna
- **Set, lights, sound and props** — building the actual show with Colton
- **Study** — off-book drilling, script analysis, character and ensemble track work

**Every show we produce carries a technical theatre track**, and it is not the consolation prize. If designing the projections for this show or calling a cue sequence turns out to be the thing that lights you up, tell us. Some of you will discover that this week.

## What to bring, every day

- **A packed lunch and a snack.** We do not provide food. No DoorDash or delivery during the camp day — this one is firm.
- **A refillable water bottle**, named.
- **A pencil.** Not a pen.
- **Your script and a binder** once we hand them out.
- **A layer.** The building runs cold.
- Anything with your name on it stands a much better chance of coming home.

## What to wear

Modest movement clothes with closed-toed shoes or dance shoes. Leggings, joggers, or athletic shorts with a fitted top or t-shirt — things that stay put when you bend, lift your arms, or lie on the floor. Hair tied back and off your face. No dangling jewellery or smartwatches. If you are on the build crew, closed-toed shoes are not a suggestion, they are the rule.

## The material, and looking after each other

We will open Monday with this conversation, together, before we read a word.

What you need to know now:

- **You can step out of any rehearsal, at any point, without explaining yourself.** Nobody will chase you or ask why in front of the room. Someone will quietly check you are all right.
- **Katie Rivers is our Director of Health and Safety.** She is a Licensed Clinical Social Worker. Going to her is not a big deal and not a betrayal of anyone.
- **If you are struggling — with the show or with anything else — tell an adult here.** That is not an imposition. That is the job.
- **988** reaches the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, by call or text, any hour. **Text HOME to 741741** for the Crisis Text Line. Put both in your phone tonight. Not because we expect you to need them, but because a number you already have is a number you might use.
- We do not joke about this subject in this room, and what happens in a rehearsal does not become a group chat.

If this material is close to something in your own life, you are not obliged to tell us why, and you are still welcome here. Tell us what you need and we will work with it.

## What we will expect from you

- **Act I off book by Monday the tenth.** Weekend homework is real homework.
- **We are in the theatre from Friday the seventh.** After that, no phones on the deck.
- **Tech week is mandatory.** Every call, all of it. Tech is the first time the show, the set, the lights and the sound exist in the same room, and it does not work if anyone is missing.
- Be on time. Be off book when you said you would be. Look after the person next to you.
