# Winter and Spring Break Theatre Camps: What They Are, and How to Choose One

**Subtitle:** A break camp is not a small summer camp. One day buys a skill, five days buys a show, and the price on the tuition line is not the number that decides which camp is cheaper.

*Originally published at https://novapa.org/blog/spring-break-theatre-camps-for-kids-tony-cimino-johnson.html*

---

The first one always arrives in October, and it is never about theatre.

"Hi — are you open on November 3?"

That is the entire email. Nothing about who is teaching, nothing about what her daughter would do for eight hours. Twenty years in, my students call me Mr. CJ, and I know what that parent is asking. She is asking whether she burns a vacation day, calls her mother, or sets a laptop on the kitchen counter and referees a seven year old between two meetings she cannot move.

For a while, that stung a little. I wanted the question to be about the work.

Then I counted our own calendar. Between the first teacher workday in October and the last day of spring break in March, the divisions around us close for roughly eighteen weekdays. Nearly four working weeks a year in which school is out and work is not.

So I stopped treating the childcare question as the lesser question.

Here is the principle I run these days on, and the one I want you to shop with: **a break camp has to answer two questions at once, and the camp that answers only one of them is the one you should not book.** A day that solves your Tuesday but bores your child is a bad day. A day that thrills your child but ends at noon is also a bad day, because you did not actually get the day. Both, or neither.

## What a break camp actually is (and what it is not)

Nobody defines this category, so parents shop with summer-camp instincts, and summer-camp instincts are wrong here. These are three different products wearing one name.

- **The single closure day** | One weekday: a teacher workday, student holiday, or federal holiday. One subject, one day, done.

- **The holiday cluster** | Two or three scattered days around Thanksgiving or the winter holidays, rarely consecutive, often a different room of children each day.

- **The full break week** | Five consecutive weekdays, almost always spring break, the only break that arrives as one unbroken block with no holiday in the middle of it.

What none of them are is a small summer camp. A two-week intensive has time: time to let a shy kid watch from the side for three days, time for a group to become a group. A break camp has none of that. Your child walks in cold at 8:30 and the day is over at 4:15. That is not a flaw, it is the design constraint, and a good break camp is built around it on purpose.

## Winter break and spring break are two different problems

They get listed together in every activity guide in America, and they should not be.

**Winter break is long, broken, and mostly dark.** Two weeks, cut in half by the holidays themselves, with family travel scattered through it. Most theatre programs simply close, and ours is among them: we run day camps on division closure days through the fall and winter, and nothing between December 20 and January 3. Faculty have families too, and a program that never stops eventually stops permanently. If you need coverage across the winter holidays, your realistic options run on volume rather than artistry: parks and recreation, gyms, science centers. Book early. That is the thinnest supply of the year.

**Spring break is short, whole, and the best theatre week on the calendar.** One clean run of five weekdays, no holiday in the middle, everybody home from wherever they went in December. If you are going to buy your child exactly one week of theatre outside of summer, buy this one. It is the only non-summer week long enough for a company of kids to build something and then perform it.

Put simply: for winter break, shop for coverage and be unembarrassed about it. For spring break, shop for the work.

## The arithmetic nobody publishes: what one day buys, what five days buy

**One day is a number.** Seven usable hours, minus lunch, minus the twenty minutes it takes a room of strangers to stop being strangers. In that window a well-run camp can stage one song, run a day of improv, build one character from nothing, or take a teenager through a single audition skill start to finish. Our twelve to fifteen year olds have spent one-day camps on cold-read clinics and self-tape boot camps, and every camper leaves with something to show at 4:15.

**Five days is a show.** Not a full-length musical, and anyone promising you one in a week is selling. But five consecutive days is enough to read a short piece on Monday, cast it, stage it, clean it, and perform it for families on Friday. That is a different experience from five unrelated days stacked together, because the kids are building one thing.

**Rule: one day, one skill. Five days, one show. Anything advertised outside that ratio is overselling, and your child will feel the gap before you do.**

Watch for the tell. A one-day camp promising "a full production" spends the afternoon teaching thirty kids to walk on and wave. A five-day camp with no Friday showing has wasted the only week of the year that could hold one.

## What spring break theatre camps cost

I am unapologetic about publishing prices, because their absence is how this industry keeps parents guessing. Almost nobody in the results you just scrolled past will give you a number before you fill in a form. The honest East Coast range, from published figures:

- **A full break week at a nonprofit theatre school** commonly runs $350 to $500 before extras. The Theatre Lab in Washington, DC publishes $450 for its spring break week, 9:00 to 3:00, with early drop-off and aftercare priced separately.

- **Single days** land between $70 and $115. [Our own day camps](https://novapa.org/day-camps.html) in Leesburg are $79, 8:30 to 4:15, in three age groups: five to nine, nine to twelve, twelve to fifteen.

- **Multi-day packs** are where the savings sit. Ours is five camp days for $349, or $69.80 a day, redeemable against any days on the calendar rather than one fixed week. Additional children take five percent off, and the [full price list](https://novapa.org/pricing.html) is public.

Now the number that actually decides which camp is cheaper, and it is not the tuition. **Compare the hours, not the price.** A $450 week running 9:00 to 3:00 with $15-a-day aftercare is $525 for a shorter day than a $395 week running 8:30 to 4:15. Parents lose this comparison constantly, because the tuition line is the one printed in bold. Divide price by hours in the building. That is your real number.

And ask about assistance before deciding you cannot afford it. Good programs have it and most are quiet about it. Ours is confidential, through [info@novapa.org](mailto:info@novapa.org); Encore Stage & Studio in Arlington offers scholarships too. Nobody volunteers this at checkout. Ask.

## Seven questions to ask before you book

Send these in one email. A program that answers all seven plainly has thought about your child. A program that answers in brochure language has not.

1. **What are the actual hours, and what does care before and after them cost?** The most expensive hidden line in break camps.

2. **How are ages grouped, and how many children are in my child's room?** "Ages 5 to 15" together on one day is crowd control, not teaching.

3. **Who is teaching, and are they teaching all five days?** Break weeks are where programs rotate in whoever is available.

4. **What happens at the end?** A showing, a performance, or nothing. Any of the three can be right. Not knowing is not.

5. **What is the plan for a child who has never done this before?** Break camps draw first-timers, because parents book them for the date. Good ones build for that.

6. **Lunch: provided or packed?** Ours is packed, and we eat together. Fine either way. Find out before 7:45 on Monday.

7. **What happens if my child is sick, or the building closes for weather?** Get it in writing. In winter this is not hypothetical.

**If two or more answers are vague, keep shopping.** You are not being difficult. You are buying a full day of your child's life and a full day of your own.

## Spring break camps in Loudoun, Fairfax, and Prince William: the 2027 dates

For families near us the dates are fixed. Loudoun County Public Schools' spring break runs **Monday, March 22 through Friday, March 26, 2027**, with Fairfax and Prince William lined up that week. Winter break runs December 20, 2026 through January 3, 2027; Thanksgiving break is November 22 through 28, 2026.

Loudoun County Parks and Recreation publishes its spring activity guide in mid-January, which is when the county's lower-cost break camps become visible; if budget is the constraint, check there first. Private programs, ours included, open spring break registration well before that, because one week has a hard cap on how many campers can be in a piece.

Our spring break week is a Spring Break Musical Theatre Camp for the nine to twelve and twelve to fifteen companies, building one show from first read to a Friday performance, with the five to nine group running its own full day alongside. It sits on the [season calendar](https://novapa.org/calendar.html) with every closure day between now and then, marked with the divisions that are out.

One local note, because it comes up every February: when a division closes for snow and our building is safe to reach, we run a Snow Day Fun Day, decided early enough to email families by 7:00 a.m. Ask any camp what their snow answer is. "We will let you know" at 8:15 is not an answer when you are already late.

## When not to book a break camp

I would rather say this than take the registration.

**Not as a first experience of theatre, if you have a choice.** A one-day camp asks a brand new child to walk into a room of strangers and be brave before lunch. Some kids love exactly that. A slow-to-warm child is better served starting in a [weekly class](https://novapa.org/classes.html), where week one can be spent watching from the side and nobody minds. Break camps then become the fun extra, not the audition.

**Not to fix something.** Camps are wonderful and they are not interventions. If the worry underneath the search is confidence, or an audition coming up, those have their own answers, and a week in March is not one of them. We do [coaching](https://novapa.org/coaching.html) for the audition question and a [two-week summer camp](https://novapa.org/summer-2027.html) for the immersive one. I would point you at either first.

**And do not feel a thing about booking one purely because school is closed and work is not.** That is not a lesser reason. That is a Tuesday in November, and a room full of kids singing is a genuinely good place for your child to spend it.

## Closing reflection: the email in October

I answer those emails the same way every year now. Yes, we are open on November 3. Here is what your daughter would do that day, here is who is teaching it, here are the hours, here is the price.

Because the parent writing at 11:40 at night with a school calendar open in another tab is not being unserious about theatre. She is doing what I do every August with a season, a budget, and a faculty roster. She is looking at a year and trying to make the pieces hold. The eighteen days are coming whether any of us plan for them or not.

And what I have watched happen, over and over, is that the day booked for the most practical reason in the world becomes the day the child talks about at dinner all week. A kid gets dropped off because an office would not close, and gets picked up having sung in front of people for the first time in her life.

Choose the camp that answers both of your questions, and let the second one surprise you.

## Questions parents ask

**What is a spring break theatre camp?**
A spring break theatre camp is a short theatre program that runs on the weekdays your school district is closed, usually Monday through Friday during a single break week, for a full school-length day. It is not a shortened summer camp. Because the week is contiguous, five days is exactly enough time to build and perform one short piece, which is why a spring break camp can end in a real showing when a one-day holiday camp cannot. Most run for ages roughly five to fifteen, grouped by age, with campers bringing their own lunch.

**How much do spring break and winter break theatre camps cost?**
Published prices on the East Coast generally run from about $70 to $115 per day. A full break week at a mid-size nonprofit theatre school commonly lands between $350 and $500 before extras; The Theatre Lab in Washington, DC lists $450 for its spring break week, with early drop-off and aftercare priced separately. Northern Virginia Performing Arts in Leesburg publishes $79 for a single camp day, or $349 for a five-day camp pack, which works out to $69.80 a day. Ask specifically whether before-care and after-care are included, because a camp that ends at 3:00 p.m. with paid aftercare is often the more expensive option.

**Is one day of theatre camp long enough to be worth it?**
Yes, but only if you buy the right thing. One day is enough to learn and perform a single number, run a full improv day, or work one audition skill start to finish. It is not enough to mount a show, and a one-day camp that promises a production is overselling. Judge a single day by whether it has one clear subject and something to show at pickup. Judge a five-day break week by whether the campers build one piece together from first read to performance.
