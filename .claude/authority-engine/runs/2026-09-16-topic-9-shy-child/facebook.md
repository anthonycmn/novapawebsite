## Post

"I'm so sorry, she's just really shy."

I've heard that sentence from a thousand doorways in twenty years, usually mouthed over a child's head while she holds the door frame like the building might tip. And I want every parent who has ever said it to know: it has never once been a problem for me or for the class. The only person it's a problem for is the child hearing it.

A shy child is almost never afraid of pretending. They run whole kingdoms under the dining room table. What they're afraid of is being watched as themselves. Drama is the one activity that hands them a character, a group, and a game to stand behind while they practice speaking up, and the courage they borrow from the wolf doesn't go back when the mask comes off.

I wrote up the whole honest answer: the difference between shy, anxious, and quiet (they look the same in the doorway and need different things), what a good first month actually looks like week by week, what to say in the car on the way home, what to walk away from, and the cases where a drama class is not the right first step.

Full guide is here: https://novapa.org/blog/is-my-child-too-shy-for-drama-class-tony-cimino-johnson.html

Bring her to the doorway. Let her hold the frame. Sit on the bench. Watch for the rain.
