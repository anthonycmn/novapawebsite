## Substack — title: "Minute Forty" · subtitle: "What you're actually buying when you pay for voice lessons, from the hiring side of the table."

When I audition a voice teacher for our faculty, I am not listening for how beautifully they sing. I am listening for what they do in minute forty of a lesson with a fourteen-year-old whose voice cracked in front of their friends that morning.

That skill is what you are actually paying for.

This week I published the guide I wish I could hand every parent who asks me what voice lessons should cost: the real national price ranges (roughly $30-60 a half hour, $60-150 an hour, and why), what separates a $120 teacher from a $40 hobbyist, the seven questions I ask before hiring anyone to teach a teenager, and the thing no lessons page will tell you - when private lessons are NOT the right first move for your kid.

The short version: you are paying for four things. Training. Adolescent-voice expertise, because teen voices are mid-construction and can be damaged by premature belting. Structure, because thirty minutes of favorite songs is karaoke with feedback. And safety, because nobody improves while performing confidence they do not have.

The best teacher is not the most famous one. It is the one your teen will be honest in front of.
