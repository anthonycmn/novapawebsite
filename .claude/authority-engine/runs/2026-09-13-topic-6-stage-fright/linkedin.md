## Post

I train every stage manager and teaching artist in our building on the same
seven steps for a frozen child in the wings. Get low. Do not ask what is
wrong. Give the body one job. Give them a job that is not about them. Talk to
the character, not the kid. Send them on with the group, never alone. And if
they still cannot, they do not go, and the show survives.

Most of that is not about children.

Last week a new teaching artist asked me why step two is on the list. Why not
ask what is wrong? Because a person at threshold does not know, and now you
have handed them a question to fail. What they need is something with muscles
and breath and an end. Press your feet into the floor. Blow out the candle.
Then a responsibility for someone else, because responsibility for another
person is the fastest exit from self-watching I have ever found.

I have watched managers do the opposite with adults for twenty years. The
employee locks up in a meeting and the manager leans in, towers, and asks
"what's going on with you?" Every one of those moves makes it worse. Get low.
Give them a task. Put them next to someone. Let them not go on this time.

Fear is loudest when a person is alone and watching themselves. It gets quiet
when they are busy and part of something. Build the room for that.

Inspiritu,
Mr. CJ
