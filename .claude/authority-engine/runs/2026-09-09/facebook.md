**Post:**

Audition season is starting for school musicals across Loudoun and Fairfax, and the question we get more than any other is some version of: what should my child sing?

So I wrote the honest answer down. Not a ranked list of impressive songs — what a director is actually listening for in the first fifteen seconds, which is a much smaller and much kinder question than most families think. Where the voice comfortably lives. Whether a child can hold pitch and tempo with a piano, which is a genuinely different skill than singing in the car. Whether they mean the words.

Nobody is scoring difficulty. A hard song sung at seventy percent reads as a warning. A simple song sung at a hundred percent reads as a professional.

The guide includes actual song recommendations for ages 6 to 9 and 10 to 13, the overdone list with honest context (short version: "Tomorrow" is not a disqualifier if your child owns it), the four questions that settle the choice, and how to cut sixteen bars without wrecking the song.

And one small request that has nothing to do with singing: after the audition, try not to ask "how did it go?" That question teaches a child the audition was a test they might have failed. Ask what the room was like. Ask who else was there. Ask if it was fun.
