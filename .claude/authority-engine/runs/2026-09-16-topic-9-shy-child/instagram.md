## Caption

Your child is not too shy for drama class.

Twenty years of first days, and the same sentence in the doorway: "I'm so sorry, she's just really shy." One hand around a parent's leg, the other holding the door frame like the building might tip.

Here is what I know. A shy child is almost never afraid of pretending. They run whole kingdoms under the dining room table. What they're afraid of is being watched as themselves. Drama hands them a character, a group, and a game to stand behind, and the courage they borrow from the wolf doesn't go back when the mask comes off.

Week one, the bench. Week two, rain in her lap during the storm game. Week three, a tree. Week six, the wolf, one line, said loud to the whole room.

She was never too shy. She was too shy to be watched alone, and nobody asked her to be.

The full guide (shy vs. anxious vs. quiet, what a good first month looks like, what to say in the car, when to wait) is at the link in bio.
