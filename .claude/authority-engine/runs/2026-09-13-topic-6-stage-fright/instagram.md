## Caption

The seahorse would not go on.

Second grader. Purple felt. Both hands around a light boom in the wing, eyes
on the floor. Her mom had been practicing with her every night and telling
her she was going to be great.

That is most of why she was holding the light boom.

Practicing the week of the show tells a kid the grown-ups are worried. "Great"
becomes the standard, so one skipped word is a failure. "Don't be nervous"
gives her two problems instead of one.

What worked: I got low. I did not ask what was wrong. I gave her one job for
her body and one job that was about somebody else. She went on holding the
crab's hand, half a beat late, and nobody in the fourth row could tell.

backstage protocol we use when a child locks up, and when it is more than
nerves.

**Slide 2:** "Three things parents call stage fright:" then stacked: Pre-show
nerves (normal, leave it alone) · The freeze (a body at threshold, not a choice) ·
Dread that starts days out (talk to your pediatrician)

**Slide 3:** "What loving adults do that makes it worse:" stacked: Rehearsing at
home the week of · "Don't be nervous" · "You're going to be great" · Watching
from the wings · The car ride debrief

**Slide 4:** "Why kids are less afraid as somebody else." Beneath: "The role is a
container. Do not talk to your child. Talk to the seahorse."

**Slide 5:** "Backstage protocol for a frozen kid:" numbered: 1 Get low · 2 Do not
ask what is wrong · 3 Give the body one job · 4 Give them a job about someone
else · 5 Talk to the character · 6 Send them on with the group · 7 If they still
cannot, they do not go

**Slide 6:** "At the stage door, say one true thing." Beneath: "'I cannot wait to
see it.' Then go find your seat and be boringly calm."

**Slide 7:** "When it is more than stage fright." Beneath: "Dread that does not
lift. Stomachaches on rehearsal days. Refusal that spreads. Call your
pediatrician, not your director."

**Slide 8 (close):** "Your child does not need to be more prepared. Your child
needs to be less alone in the wing, less watched, and given a job." Signed Mr.
CJ. Link in bio.
