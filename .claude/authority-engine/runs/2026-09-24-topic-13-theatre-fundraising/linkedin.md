The first budget I was handed for a theatre program was five thousand dollars.

Not for a show. For a year — scripts, rights, lumber, paint, and microphones that were already broken when I got there. I did the arithmetic in a classroom that wasn't finished yet, as a roving teacher with a cart, and reached the obvious conclusion. We'd have to raise money.

So we did what everyone does. Catalogs. A car wash. The discount card nobody wanted to buy twice. We raised a bit. I spent my Saturdays on it. And the next September the budget was still five thousand dollars.

That program eventually ran on something closer to three quarters of a million, and I want to be honest about what got it there. It wasn't the car wash, and it wasn't working harder.

It was giving up on fundraising.

Fundraising is asking people for money and handing them something incidental — cookie dough, a clean car. It arrives once, late, with a vendor's cut removed, and it builds nothing. Next year you start at zero.

Revenue is money the program earns by doing what it already does. Tickets priced honestly. Programs and sponsorship. A building used in the summer instead of sitting dark. It arrives on a schedule, it scales with the work, and it's the same activity twice.

Here's the test I'd offer anyone running a program. Take a fundraiser's net profit after the vendor's cut. Count every hour it costs YOU — emails, envelopes, chasing the families who didn't return theirs, sorting frozen food in a hallway. Divide.

If that hourly rate is lower than the work it displaced, it isn't a fundraiser. It's a transfer of your life into the program's bank account at a terrible exchange rate.

Run it honestly and most catalog drives land south of minimum wage, paid by the least replaceable person in the building. Meanwhile one fall musical commonly nets four to six thousand in box office.

Programs don't fail because they run out of money. They fail because the teacher runs out. Money problems are recoverable. A teacher who leaves in year four is not, and the program usually goes with them.
