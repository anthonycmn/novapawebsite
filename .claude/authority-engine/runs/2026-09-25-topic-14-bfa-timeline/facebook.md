If you have a senior thinking about a musical theatre degree, this is the week to read something.

Every year around now I get the same email, and it always has the same shape. "She decided in August that this is what she wants to do. Are we already too late?"

The answer is no. But here's the part that catches almost everybody out, and it's worth knowing today rather than in January:

Most BFA programs require a prescreen — recorded songs, a monologue, often a dance reel — and those deadlines land between November 1 and December 15. A few are late October. The schools watch those recordings and decide who gets a live audition at all.

So the audition season people picture, the one with hotel ballrooms in January and February, is downstream of a recording made in October. Nearly every family I've watched panic in January was panicking about the wrong month.

We put the whole year up as two timelines: one for the student, one for the parent. The parent track is the one nobody writes down, and it's mostly money and logistics — set the budget before anyone falls in love with a school, book midwinter travel in the fall, and plan for the December night when the first results land during the holidays.

And there's a section at the end for families starting late, with what to cut and in what order. Short version: cut the number of schools before you cut the quality of anything.

Read it here: https://novapa.org/blog/musical-theatre-college-audition-timeline-tony-cimino-johnson.html
