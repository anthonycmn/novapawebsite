## The Rubric Does Not Have a Column for the High Note

Every September I write an eleven-line audition notice, and every September I
watch families read into it things it never said. That gap taught me something
about rubrics that I now apply to every hiring panel and every leadership
decision I sit on.

## What the sheet actually scores

Parents imagine a column for the high note. There is not one. What the sheet
in front of me actually has, in roughly this order, is: did the student
listen; could they take an adjustment in the room and change something; what
did they do at the dance call while being bad at it in front of peers; how did
they treat the nervous kid in the hallway and the volunteer at sign-in; and
could they walk in and out like a person.

None of that is the song. All of it is the next eleven weeks. I am not casting
a recording. I am choosing the thirty-five people I will be in a room with
every afternoon until December, and the thing that predicts that room is not
the thing the family spent two weeks drilling.

## The reverse mistake

I have sat on the other side of hiring panels and watched the exact error run
backwards. The rubric scores the prepared answer, the polished deck, the
credentials, the high note. It does not score what happened when the question
came sideways. It does not score how the candidate spoke to the person who
brought the water. And then the team spends a year finding out what the rubric
did not measure.

The fix is not a better high-note column. It is putting the actual criteria on
the sheet. When I moved "takes an adjustment" and "hallway behavior" from my
instincts onto the printed rubric, two things happened: my casting got more
defensible, and my students started practicing the things that mattered
instead of the things that were visible.

## The three lines that changed my notices

I now put three sentences on every audition notice that were never there before.

Not getting a callback does not mean not getting cast; a callback is a question
I still have. You will be asked to change something in the room; that is the
point, not a correction. And: the dance call is not about the dancing.

Families read those and prepare differently. They stop drilling and start
practicing being coachable. The rooms got better the year I wrote them down.

Whatever you run, put the real criteria on the sheet. The people who benefit
most are the ones who never had anyone to explain what the room was actually
for.
