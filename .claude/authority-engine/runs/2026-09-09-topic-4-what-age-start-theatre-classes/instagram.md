## Caption

He was nine, and he held two hundred people before he said a word.

Red felt crown. Blue gloves to the elbow. Knee pads under the costume, because
Lord Farquaad spends the whole show on his knees and nobody tells you that until
you're already cast. He threw his arms open, held it four counts longer than we
ever rehearsed, and the room went up.

Eight months earlier his mom stood in our lobby and asked me if he was too young
to start.

Here's the honest answer, and it isn't the one you'll find online. Most kids are
ready for a first theatre class between 5 and 7 — if that class is play, not
technique. Real training lands between 8 and 10. And starting at 12, or 15, is
not late.

That "ages 8 to 11" number you keep seeing? That's a casting statistic. It's
about when a kid can book work. It was never about when a kid should learn.

Forget the birthday. Ask if they can separate from you for an hour, follow a
direction from an adult who isn't you, wait through someone else's turn, pretend
without being asked, and actually want to go. Four out of five, sign them up.

The right age is the age the room was built for.

around here publishes prices — link in bio.

— Mr. CJ
