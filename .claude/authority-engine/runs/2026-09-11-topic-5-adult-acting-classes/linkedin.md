## Post

A federal contractor joined our Tuesday-night adult acting class last fall. He
presents to two hundred people at a time for a living. He told me on night one
that he had never once felt present while doing it.

Eight weeks later he said something I have been thinking about since: "I
stopped bracing."

That is the whole lesson, and it applies far outside a theatre studio.

Most adults walk into any room already managing how they are perceived. It is
a highly developed system, built over decades, and it costs a fortune in
attention. The presenter who cannot feel present is not under-prepared. He is
over-defended. Every ounce of attention he spends monitoring himself is an
ounce he cannot spend on the people in front of him.

An acting class does not teach presence by explaining it. It builds a room
where managing your image is impossible and nothing bad happens, and then it
lets you find that out for yourself, one ridiculous game at a time. The system
comes down because it stops being needed.

I run programs for five-year-olds through adults, and the adults are the ones
who need the room most. Children have not built the armor yet. Adults are
carrying it into every meeting they walk into.

If you lead people, ask yourself what your team is bracing against when you
enter the room. Then ask what it would take for them to stop.

Fix the room, not the people.

Inspiritu,
Mr. CJ
